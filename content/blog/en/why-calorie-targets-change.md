Most people set a calorie target once and never look at it again. Bodies change, schedules change and activity levels change, so a target is more useful when it's reviewed regularly.

## Why targets drift

As your weight changes, your energy needs change too. Activity matters as well: a busy month at work can lower your daily movement, and a few weeks of training can raise it. A target that stays fixed may stop matching what you're doing.

## Signs it's time to review

- Your weight has stayed the same for several weeks, despite following the plan.
- You feel constantly hungry or unusually tired.
- Your routine has changed, for example a new job, a new training schedule or a new season.

## How to review it

1. **Track consistently for two weeks.** Log meals and keep portions as accurate as you can.
2. **Look at the trend, not one day.** Daily weight moves for many reasons, so use weekly averages.
3. **Make a small change.** Adjust by a modest amount, then watch the trend for another two weeks.

## A note on safety

Very low calorie targets are not suitable for everyone. If you're pregnant, managing a health condition or taking medication, speak to your doctor before you change your intake.
