Planning meals around an allergy is easier when you do it once for the whole week instead of deciding at every meal. This guide walks through a simple routine you can repeat.

## Start with a clear list of triggers

Write down every food you need to avoid, including less obvious sources. Milk can hide in bread, sauces and some plant-based drinks. Gluten can appear in soy sauce and stock cubes. Keep the list on your phone so you can check it in the supermarket.

## Read labels every time

Manufacturers change recipes, so a product that was safe last month may not be safe today. Check the ingredients and the allergen statement on every pack, even for items you buy often.

> If an allergy is severe, follow the advice of your doctor or allergist. A meal plan supports that advice; it doesn't replace it.

## Batch-cook a few safe staples

Choose three or four foods you already know are safe, such as plain rice, roasted vegetables, grilled chicken and a simple sauce. Cook them in one session and combine them differently across the week. This cuts the number of decisions you make and the number of labels you need to read.

## Plan swaps before you shop

Most allergy-friendly recipes work with a simple swap. Use this table as a starting point:

| Usual ingredient | Common swap | Check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cow's milk | Oat or soy drink | Oat drinks may contain gluten |
| Wheat flour | Rice or oat flour | Oat flour can be cross-contaminated |
| Soy sauce | Coconut aminos | Confirm the label for soy and wheat |
| Butter | Olive oil or a dairy-free spread | Some spreads contain milk |

## Build the shopping list from the plan

Once the seven dinners and the breakfasts are set, write the list by aisle. Buying from a single list means fewer impulse items, and fewer chances to pick up a product you haven't checked.

## Review the week on Sunday

Each Sunday, look at what you ate, what you threw away and what you wish you had prepared. Small changes add up: one more safe breakfast you enjoy is worth more than a perfect plan you won't follow.

*This article is general guidance and not medical advice. Always check product labels for severe allergies.*
